How it works

Tell it what you want to learn. It takes it from there.

  1. 1

    Tell Veya what and why.

    "I want to finally get statistics." "I have an exam in three weeks." Whatever it is.

  2. 2

    It builds your path.

    A personal curriculum, with each topic getting its own space.

  3. 3

    It talks you through it.

    Live voice you can interrupt, question and answer back. Like a patient friend who happens to know the subject.

  4. 4

    It remembers you.

    Veya keeps track of what you understand and where you stall, then decides what's next and makes the first move. Every session builds on the last.

Why voice

Learning shouldn't need a desk.

Voice first, because it's personal and it fits into the life you already have: the bus, the walk, the queue. Audio is where we start, not where we stop. Practice, projects and proof of what you can do come next.

Where it came from

It started with a course I nearly failed.

I had a heavy AI course and way too much reading before exams. So I turned the material into audio and learned on the go, the way I listen to music and podcasts. I passed.

Then friends asked if they could pay for it. That's when I asked the real question: why does the learner always have to adapt to the format?
